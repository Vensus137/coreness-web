The panel, room by room

01 Agent team A ceiling on teammates, and two switches on the policy the Lead starts with.

More hands, and a second pair of eyes.

The ceiling decides how many teammates a session may carry. The first switch hands the Lead the product's paragraph on leading a team: it takes the place of one sentence in what the harness tells it, and every other line stays as written.

The second adds the review pass: a fresh reviewer holds each round of work against the spec, asks what is weak, what is extra and what is missing — and edits nothing. The Lead decides where to call one, and a small task needs none.

The team is the harness's; Coreness sets the ceiling and the two switches, and reports what its patch did.

02 MCP servers Every server on one card — its tools, its switch, its sign-in — set up in the panel rather than by hand in a file.

Your MCP servers, without the fiddling.

One card per server: the transport, the command or address, the environment it needs and the tools the harness found — read from the profile, so what you see is what runs.

The switch is the whole edit. Turning a server on writes the row into the profile itself, not a copy; the harness owns the connection, and the card shows what the save left behind — if the read-back disagrees, it says Not saved.

A server behind OAuth is signed in from its card: the token goes in as a credential reference, not a value.

The harness's Plugins page cannot edit these rows. This panel is their editor.

03 Qoder Your Qoder subscription, registered natively: its models, levels, credits and data policy.

Your Qoder plan, plugged in.

Qoder is its own product, with its own subscription — not ours. Coreness registers it as a provider, so the plan you already pay for serves the agent here: paste the token once and it goes into the harness's credential store — never a config file, never a log.

Both regions work out of the box — global and China; the one that answers is kept, and a region can be pinned. Your account's models arrive with their reasoning levels, as Qoder publishes them.

The spend stays in sight: credits and campaigns are read while the panel runs, sharing is one press away, and Qoder's web search is a switch — off until you turn it on.

A failed read says what failed. No stand-in value is drawn under it.

04 Rate limiter A ceiling per model that makes a call wait for room instead of failing.

A limit that waits, not refuses.

Set a ceiling in tokens per minute and calls that would cross it wait their turn instead of failing: the gate holds a queue and lets the call at its head through as room appears.

Past the longest wait the call goes anyway, marked forced — a full window is a delay with a name, not a hung turn. Nothing is limited until you set a limit; everything else passes through untouched.

The counters say their own scope: this run alone. A reload keeps them, a restart wipes them, and spend elsewhere is not counted.

Window
60 s — the stretch the ceiling is counted over.
Longest wait
60 s — how long a call may be held before it goes forced.
Caution
0.9 — the share of the ceiling the gate runs at.
Ceiling
Tokens per minute, declared per provider and model.

The defaults; each one is set on the Rate limiter page.

No small print

The fine points, in plain sight.

No service

Nothing runs on our side — no cloud, no account, no service between you and the profile it reads.

No version bound

Plain files, no build step, and no Harness version bound — it installs into the one you run.

Only what you use

Each part is its own plugin. A profile switches off what it does not use on the app's Plugins page.

One owner

A local tool: one owner, one machine, no shared database, no service of its own.

MIT

The source is the install. Read every line before you run it.

No sign-up

No account with us and no key of ours: the install is the whole onboarding.

Install

Two steps, in the app's own window.

01
Open Plugins → Add plugin in DeepSeek Harness.
02
Paste the address.
github:Vensus137/Coreness-DSHThe address

It installs into the profile the app is already running — you do not choose one. The app asks for a restart only when it cannot apply the change in place, and nothing is built on the way in.

By command line

dsh is the application's command: Application → Manage dsh Command… puts it on the path. A terminal opened before that keeps the path it started with.

$dsh plugin --profile desktop add github:Vensus137/Coreness-DSH

On the desktop application the profile is named desktop; on the web surface it is named web. A profile that does not exist yet is created.

Updates through the market

Updates come through the app's Discover tab: switch Custom plugin markets on once under Plugins → More, add https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vensus137/Coreness-DSH/main/market.json, and the plugin stands there with its versions — install and update from that page.

To pin a fixed version, name its tag where an address is taken: github:Vensus137/Coreness-DSH#<tag>.
